## Summary: where policy actually stands

As of November 2026 the United States has **no enacted federal policy aimed at AI-driven job loss**. Federal action in 2025-26 ran the other way on the safety net (the July 2025 reconciliation law cut Medicaid and SNAP by over $1.2 trillion over ten years) and focused AI policy on speed, infrastructure and blocking state regulation (the July 2025 AI Action Plan; Executive Order 14365 of December 2025). The worker-facing parts of the Action Plan are training, data collection and pilots under existing law. The one new universal asset programme is "Trump accounts" ($1,000 for each child born 2025-28), which is capital at age 18, not income.

What changed in 2026 is the politics. A wave of bills appeared: a Sanders bill to take 50% of large AI companies into a public fund paying about $1,000 a person a year; House and Senate Democratic bills for token taxes, an excess-profits tax and expanded unemployment insurance; and a bipartisan Hawley-Warner layoff-reporting bill. None has had a committee vote. The President himself floated federal equity stakes in OpenAI, Anthropic and xAI (June 2026), OpenAI reportedly offered 5%, and Trump promised a $5,000 "dividend" per adult if Republicans held Congress. They did not: Democrats won the House and Senate on 3 November 2026, and voters backed limits on data centers in 40 of 41 local measures.

States are ahead of Congress, but what they have passed is **disclosure and procedure** (California's "No Robo Bosses" law and AI-layoff notices), not redistribution. No state has enacted a robot or token tax. Five states ban local guaranteed-income programmes.

Public opinion is strongly negative on AI and jobs (71-84% expect harm to workers) yet lukewarm on unconditional cash. The measured labour market is still calm: unemployment was 4.1% in October 2026. So the debate is being conducted on expectation, not on realised mass displacement, and the fiscal arithmetic of the larger proposals remains unresolved.

## 1. US federal action, 2025-2026

### 1.1 Executive branch: AI strategy and pre-emption

- **America's AI Action Plan (July 2025).** Three pillars (innovation, infrastructure, international). The worker section calls the agenda "worker-first" and recommends: making AI skills a priority in existing education and workforce funding; Treasury guidance so employers can reimburse AI training tax-free under Internal Revenue Code section 132; BLS, Census and BEA study of AI's labour effects using existing surveys; an **AI Workforce Research Hub** at the Labor Department; use of existing discretionary funds for rapid retraining of people displaced by AI; and state pilots under existing workforce law. It proposes no new income support, no new entitlement and no new tax. https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Americas-AI-Action-Plan.pdf [opened]
  - The Hub had not launched as of mid-2026 according to press reports. [secondary] A Senate bill (below) would require it by statute. https://rollcall.com/2026/07/29/lawmakers-want-better-data-on-ais-workforce-impacts/ [opened]
- **Earlier 2025 orders cited by the Plan:** EO 14277 (AI education for youth) and EO 14278 (skilled trades), both 23 April 2025. Same PDF [opened].
- **Pre-emption of state AI laws.**
  - A 10-year moratorium on state AI laws was removed from the 2025 reconciliation bill by a 99-1 Senate vote in July 2025. https://www.techtimes.com/articles/329592/20261027/record-115-state-ai-laws-2026-illinois-sets-frontier-audit-mandate-congress-still-silent.htm [opened]
  - A later attempt to attach pre-emption to the FY2026 defence authorisation bill was dropped. [secondary]
  - **Executive Order 14365, 11 December 2025**: creates a Justice Department AI Litigation Task Force to challenge state AI laws (30-day deadline); tells Commerce to list "onerous" state laws (90 days); makes states with such laws ineligible for some broadband (BEAD non-deployment) money; asks the FCC and FTC to consider pre-emptive standards; and orders a legislative proposal for a single national framework. Proposed carve-outs: child safety, data-center siting, and states' own use of AI. https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/eliminating-state-law-obstruction-of-national-artificial-intelligence-policy/ [opened]
  - The task force was set up on 10 January 2026 and has sued on dormant-commerce-clause grounds; no court ruling had been reported by late October 2026. TechTimes URL above [opened]. First DOJ intervention reported 24 April 2026. [secondary]
  - **White House National Policy Framework for AI, 20 March 2026**: seven areas of recommended legislation. On work it asks only for "non-regulatory" federal support for AI training through existing systems and more study of AI's effect on job tasks. It asks Congress to pre-empt state laws that impose undue burdens, while preserving general consumer, child-protection and zoning law. Summary by the National Governors Association, 25 March 2026: https://www.nga.org/?p=78319 [opened]
  - A bipartisan House discussion draft, the "Great American AI Act" (June 2026), is stalled. Congress has enacted **no** federal AI statute. Meanwhile states enacted 115 AI laws in 30 states between 1 January and 15 October 2026, up from 73 in 27 states in 2025 (Transparency Coalition count, 27 October 2026). TechTimes URL above [opened]
- **June 2026 executive order** creating a process to vet advanced AI systems for national-security risk for up to a month before release. Reported by AP, 11 June 2026: https://fortune.com/2026/06/11/anthropic-dario-amodei-ai-jobs-tax-displacement-fund/ [opened]

### 1.4 The sovereign wealth fund order and federal equity stakes

- **Order of 3 February 2025** told Treasury and Commerce to deliver a plan for a US sovereign wealth fund within 90 days. https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/02/a-plan-for-establishing-a-united-states-sovereign-wealth-fund/ [opened]
- No fund with a statute, mandate or governance has been created. A July 2026 commentary describes instead a deal-by-deal portfolio assembled through Commerce, Defense and the Development Finance Corporation: Intel (about 9.9%, from converted CHIPS Act grants), MP Materials (about 15%), Lithium Americas (10%), Trilogy Metals (10%), USA Rare Earth (about 10%), and positions in quantum and chip firms; it values the CHIPS conversions at over $35 billion. https://www.softwareseni.com/beyond-intel-when-the-us-government-becomes-a-routine-shareholder/ [opened] (a commentary site; treat the valuations as approximate). Intel deal size was about $8.9 billion in August 2025. [secondary]
- The government also holds a "golden share" in U.S. Steel (2025), and about 20 CHIPS-funded companies now have some federal ownership position. https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/scaling-sovereign-wealth--evaluating-mechanisms-for-the-u.s.-government-to-own-parts-of-ai-companies [opened]
- **AI companies.** In early June 2026 President Trump said the government might take equity stakes in OpenAI, Anthropic and xAI and ruled out nationalisation. [secondary] On 2 July 2026 the Financial Times reported that OpenAI had proposed giving the government a 5% stake (about $42.6 billion at its March 2026 valuation of $852 billion), with other labs expected to do likewise; Anthropic was reported not to be in such talks. No deal was confirmed. https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/02/openai-proposes-us-government-own-5percent-stake-to-address-political-blowback.html [opened]
  - Reported positions inside the administration: Commerce Secretary Lutnick favours a sovereign-fund route; Treasury Secretary Bessent favours using company equity to fund Trump accounts. Fortune 18 June URL above [opened]
  - Lawfare's legal assessment: voting common stock runs into Delaware fiduciary law and budget risk; a revenue share plus a golden share is more workable. Lawfare URL above [opened]

### 1.5 Trump accounts

- Created by the July 2025 reconciliation law. Treasury deposits $1,000 for each US-citizen child born 2025 through 2028. Others may add up to $5,000 a year; employers up to $2,500 of that. Funds must track a US stock index; no withdrawals before 18; then treated like a traditional IRA. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Big_Beautiful_Bill_Act [opened]
- Launched 4 July 2026. By 7 October 2026 the White House reported accounts voluntarily opened for about 8 million children, then announced automatic enrolment of over 60 million more; $4.5 billion deposited in total, of which $1.3 billion from Treasury and about $2.6 billion philanthropic (the Dell gift of $250 each for up to 25 million children in lower-income ZIP codes is the largest). White House projection: $1,000 grows to about $6,000 by age 18 with no additions. https://ctmirror.org/2026/10/07/trump-accounts-auto-enrolled/ [opened]
- Scale check (my arithmetic): $1,000 × roughly 3.6 million births a year is about $3.6 billion a year, or about 0.05% of federal outlays.

### 1.6 Dividend and rebate-cheque proposals

- 9 November 2025: Trump promised a tariff "dividend" of at least $2,000 per person excluding high incomes. Ten months later nothing had been paid and no bill had been sent. https://abcnews.com/Politics/trump-promised-2000-tariff-dividends-materialized-10-months/story?id=136329468 [opened]
- 9 September 2026: at the Republican midterm convention in Dallas, Trump promised $5,000 to every adult citizen if Republicans kept both chambers. Same ABC URL [opened]. Estimated cost about $1.25 trillion. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_elections [opened]
- Context: the Supreme Court struck down a set of tariffs; $166 billion was owed in refunds, of which about $100 billion had been paid by end-July 2026 (ABC URL [opened]). Customs receipts for all of fiscal 2026 were $173 billion (Treasury Monthly Treasury Statement, September 2026: https://api.fiscaldata.treasury.gov/services/api/fiscal_service/v1/accounting/mts/mts_table_4?filter=record_date:eq:2026-09-30 [opened]). The $5,000 promise therefore equals roughly seven years of customs revenue at the FY2026 rate (my arithmetic).
- Sen. Hawley's American Worker Rebate Act (tariff rebates) has not left committee. [secondary]

### 1.7 Unemployment insurance, Medicaid, SNAP

- **Unemployment insurance:** no federal expansion enacted. The Wyden bill above is the main proposal.
- **Reconciliation law of 4 July 2025 (P.L. 119-21):** over $1.2 trillion in spending cuts, mostly Medicaid and SNAP. Medicaid: adults 19-64 must show 80 hours a month of work, volunteering or school; eligibility re-checked every six months. SNAP: federal funding down $186 billion over 2025-34; work requirement extended to ages 18-64; states with high error rates pay up to 15% of benefits. CBO: 10.9 million more uninsured; deficits up $2.8 trillion by 2034; by 2034 the bottom tenth of households lose 3.1% of income and the top tenth gain 2.7%. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Big_Beautiful_Bill_Act [opened]
- States must apply the Medicaid work requirement by 1 January 2027. [secondary] One advocacy group reports SNAP participation down 10% between July 2025 and March 2026. [secondary]

## 2. State and local action

### 2.1 Displacement, notice and "human in the loop"

- **California, signed around 30 September 2026:**
  - SB 947, "No Robo Bosses Act": employers may not rely solely on an automated system to discipline or fire; if they rely on it primarily, a human must corroborate, and the worker gets written notice and access to the data used. Operative 1 July 2027; $500 per violation; no private right to sue. Gov. Newsom vetoed a broader version in October 2025.
  - SB 951: when a layoff already covered by the state WARN law is caused wholly or substantially by AI or automation, the notice must give the number, occupations and locations affected, the functions automated and the technology used. From 1 January 2027; the state must publish quarterly summaries.
  - AB 1883: bans AI workplace tools that infer emotion or collect neural data.
  - https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/10/california-enacts-new-laws-governing-employer-use-of-ai [opened]; https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/30/california-gavin-newsom-ai-ban.html [opened]
- **Other states (status as of August 2026):** twelve measures in eight states. Connecticut SB 5 enacted (a study of AI's workforce effects and how to track AI layoffs). New York AB 9581/SB 8706 (annual reporting of AI's effect on hiring by firms over 100 employees) passed and awaited the governor. New Jersey A 5316/S 4458 (90 days' notice for AI layoffs of more than 25 people, 26 extra weeks of unemployment benefit, a retention tax credit) proposed. Minnesota HF 4369 (90 days' continued wages and retraining) and WARN amendments in Pennsylvania and Illinois under consideration. https://www.multistate.ai/updates/vol-108-ai-workforce-displacement-legislation [opened]
- New York has asked employers filing WARN notices since March 2025 whether automation caused the layoff; reportedly none said so in the first year. [secondary]
- **Human review in specific decisions, 2026 laws:** eight states (AL, CO, GA, IL, IN, IA, UT, WA) require a human reviewer for adverse health-insurance decisions; five (CO, ME, RI, TN, VT) restrict AI-delivered mental-health therapy. TechTimes URL in 1.1 [opened]

### 2.2 Automation, robot and token taxes

- **No state or city has enacted one.**
- New York A 3719, "Robot Tax Act": surcharge on a corporation equal to the state and local taxes that had been paid on a displaced employee's final year of wages. In Assembly Ways and Means; last action 7 January 2026. https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/A3719 [opened]
- New Jersey A 5676, introduced 19 October 2026: 10% tax on the market value of AI computing "tokens" processed, revenue to an AI Workforce Impact Transition Fund for retraining. https://pub.njleg.state.nj.us/Bills/2026/A6000/5676_I1.HTM [opened]
- Mississippi HB 1810: up to $10,000 per position displaced by AI at firms over 50 employees; no traction. multistate.ai URL above [opened]
- Philadelphia: a council bill (reported 30 October 2026) to tax automated services formerly done by people. [secondary]

### 2.3 Autonomous vehicles

- "Driver-in" bills (a human operator required in heavy autonomous trucks) are the Teamsters' main state campaign. Governors of California and Colorado have each vetoed one twice (Colorado most recently in 2026). Similar bills failed in Delaware, Indiana, Maryland and Texas in 2025; Alaska's passed the House unanimously and stalled in the Senate. Teamsters California sued the state DMV in August 2026 over new rules allowing driverless heavy vehicles. https://azcapitoltimes.com/news/2026/08/19/autonomous-truck-deployment-prompts-pushback-as-states-write-rules/ [opened]
- Deployment continues: Aurora began driverless night runs on the Dallas-Houston route in August 2026 (daytime since May 2025). A federal bill (H.R. 4661, Fong) would pre-empt state driver-in laws; parts were folded into a House transportation bill. A March 2026 poll for a safety group found 85% concerned about sharing roads with driverless trucks. Same URL [opened]

### 2.5 Guaranteed income

- Five states ban local guaranteed-income programmes: Arkansas, Idaho, Iowa, Kansas, South Dakota; bills pending in Illinois, North Carolina, Wisconsin. https://www.supportdemocracy.org/the-latest/2026-mid-session-trends-in-state-preemption [opened]

- California Proposition 40 (a 5% tax on billionaires) stood at 43.7% yes with 83% of precincts partly reporting on 4 November 2026; Sergey Brin gave $102 million to a group opposing it. Preliminary, not final. CNBC live URL above [opened]

## 3. Organised labour

What unions have won is mostly **consent, notice, bargaining rights and staffing floors**, not bans, and not a share of the gains.

- **Writers (WGA), 2023 contract** (term 25 September 2023 to 1 May 2026): AI cannot write or rewrite literary material or count as source material; companies cannot require writers to use AI and must disclose AI-generated material; the guild reserves the right to object to training on writers' work. https://www.wgacontract2023.org/the-campaign/summary-of-the-2023-wga-mba [opened]
- **WGA 2026 contract** (ratified April 2026, four years): studios must notify the guild if they license scripts for AI training and hold regular consultations; no payment for training use. One trade outlet reads this as weaker AI terms accepted alongside $321 million for the health plan. https://www.redsharknews.com/sag-aftra-ai-deal-synthetic-actors-2026 [opened] (single source for the WGA terms)
- **Actors (SAG-AFTRA) 2026 TV/theatrical contract** (four years; ratified with 91.4% yes on 19.3% turnout): a synthetic performer may replace a role a member could play only if it brings "significant additional value", a term left undefined; producers must notify and bargain, and the union can arbitrate for damages; a proposed fee into a compensation fund for each synthetic use (nicknamed the "Tilly tax") was not adopted; the union cannot strike over the issue until 2030. https://www.thewrap.com/industry-news/tech/sag-aftra-ai-protections-in-new-contract-synthetic/ [opened]; redsharknews URL above [opened]
- **Video-game performers (SAG-AFTRA):** strike 26 July 2024 to 9 July 2025, about 2,600 performers; won consent and disclosure for digital replicas and the right to suspend consent during a strike; ratified 95%. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%932025_SAG-AFTRA_video_game_strike [opened]
- **Dockworkers (ILA, East and Gulf coasts):** three-day strike 1-3 October 2024, over 47,000 members at 36 ports. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_port_strike [opened] Six-year contract: 62% raise; no "fully automated" equipment; new technology only by agreement; one added job per semi-automated crane. https://labornotes.org/2025/01/longshore-deal-secures-new-automation-language-and-big-pay-bump [opened] Ratified early 2025 by a reported 99%. [secondary]
- **Teamsters:** legislative strategy (section 2.3) plus litigation; no state win yet on driver-in trucks.
- **Nurses:** about 15,000 New York City nurses struck in January 2026 and won contract language against some uses of AI; National Nurses United published principles in 2025 opposing algorithms that set staffing or order tests. https://labornotes.org/2026/03/four-union-strategies-fight-ai [opened]
- **Public sector and health:** Oregon AFSCME's 2025-27 state contract bans AI for monitoring, hiring, evaluation or discipline, requires human-in-the-loop oversight and 90 days' notice of any new system. Kaiser's union alliance (22 unions, 60,000 members) won a joint technology task force and notice of monitoring tools. https://nwlaborpress.org/2026/09/ai-meet-cba/ [opened]
- **Newsrooms:** more than 50 NewsGuild contracts restrict AI. A McClatchy contract (June 2026) bans using AI to replace staff, cut pay or reduce hours. ProPublica staff struck in April 2026 and ratified a first contract in July with AI-transparency terms. Same URL [opened]
- **Manufacturing:** Machinists at Bath Iron Works (4,400 workers) won notice at the "conceptual stage" of any new technology including AI and robotics (August 2026); the union has model AI language for airline talks. https://www.nwaonline.com/news/2026/sep/27/unions-combat-ai-as-risk-to-workers/ [opened]
- **Inventory:** UC Berkeley's Lisa Kresge has catalogued more than 175 contracts with technology or AI guardrails. NW Labor Press URL above [opened]
- **AFL-CIO:** "Workers First Initiative on AI" launched 15 October 2025; a bipartisan letter in April 2026 urged applying its principles to federal procurement. [secondary]
- **OpenAI's own paper** (April 2026) proposes that workers formally help decide where AI is deployed. (Section 5.)

Pattern worth noting for the book: the strongest clause anywhere is the ILA's ban on full automation, won by a union with a physical chokepoint. Unions without a chokepoint got notice and consultation. A four-year no-strike clause (SAG-AFTRA) is long relative to the speed of the technology.

## 6. The policy menu, proponents, evidence and objections

| Option | Who proposes it | Evidence or argument for | Main objections |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Universal basic income** | Yang; Musk; Amodei (conditional on large permanent displacement); UK minister Stockwood | Simple, covers people with no work history (UI does not); experiments show modest work reductions (section 7) | Gross cost (section 8); sends more to middle-income childless adults than current programmes do (Hoynes and Rothstein 2019: https://www.nber.org/papers/w25538 [opened]); does not replace status and purpose (Hinton); public prefers earned support (4.1) |
| **Universal basic capital / citizens' dividend from a public fund** | Altman 2021; OpenAI 2026; Sanders 2026; Buttigieg; El-Sayed; Trump (equity stakes) | Alaska precedent since 1982; gives a claim on capital income as labour income shrinks; no annual appropriation fight if endowed | Most AI firms are unprofitable, so a dividend must come from selling shares or from the Treasury (Lawfare, 1.4); governance conflicts when the state is owner and regulator; valuation risk falls on citizens |
| **Asset accounts at birth** | Trump accounts (enacted); Connecticut baby bonds [secondary] (memory); Anthropic fund priority | Already law; compounding | $1,000 at birth is small, about $6,000 at 18 by the White House's own projection (1.5); voluntary top-ups favour richer families |
| **Job guarantee** | Tcherneva, Wray, Kelton and co-authors (Levy Institute, April 2018: $15 an hour plus benefits: https://www.levyinstitute.org/publications/public-service-employment-a-path-to-full-employment [opened]); Anthropic fund lists guaranteed-jobs pilots | Addresses purpose as well as income; fits the public's preference for earned support | If machines do the work better, guaranteed jobs become disguised transfers; administration at scale is untested |
| **Wage subsidies, wage insurance, retention credits** | Amodei 2026; New Jersey bill; American Compass and Mercatus ideas listed by Anthropic | Keeps people attached to work during transition | Only helps while human labour is still worth nearly its wage; subsidises employers |
| **Shorter work week** | Sanders (32 hours); OpenAI (pilots) | UK pilot June-December 2022: 61 firms, about 2,900 workers; 56 continued; revenue roughly flat; resignations down 57% (https://autonomy.work/portfolio/uk4dwpilotresults/ [opened]) | Self-selected firms; spreads work but does not create labour demand |
| **Robot, compute or token taxes** | Gates; Sanders; Jacobs-Casar; Kelly; NJ and NY bills; Korinek and Lockwood on token versus compute taxes (as summarised by Anthropic, 5) | The US tax code favours capital over labour and so over-encourages automation; fixing that would raise employment by an estimated 4% (Acemoglu, Manera, Restrepo 2020: https://www.nber.org/papers/w27052 [opened]). A robot tax is optimal while today's routine workers are still working, then should fall to zero (Guerreiro, Rebelo, Teles: https://www.nber.org/papers/w23806 [opened]). Distributional concerns justify a non-zero tax, but the optimal rate may fall as automation deepens (Costinot and Werning: https://www.nber.org/papers/w25103 [opened]) | "Robot" is hard to define; slows productivity; activity can move abroad; the 2017 European Parliament rejected one, and South Korea's 2017 measure only trimmed an automation tax credit (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robot_tax [opened]); today's base is small (section 8) |
| **Higher taxes on capital, wealth, land, consumption** | Acemoglu and Johnson (equalise tax on labour and on equipment and software: https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/fandd/issues/2023/12/Rebalancing-AI-Acemoglu-Johnson [opened]); Saez and Zucman (wealth); Altman (land); Korinek and Stiglitz (in principle non-distorting taxes can compensate losers: https://www.nber.org/papers/w24174 [opened]) | Follows the income to where it goes; land cannot move | Capital and people are mobile (section 8); valuation; consumption taxes are regressive without a rebate |
| **Windfall clause** | O'Keefe, Cihon, Garfinkel, Flynn, Leung, Dafoe (Jan 2020): firms pre-commit to give away a share of very large profits (https://www.governance.ai/research-paper/the-windfall-clause-distributing-the-benefits-of-ai-for-the-common-good [opened]) | Cheap to promise before the windfall | Voluntary and hard to enforce; no lab has signed one that I could find |
| **Broad-based ownership** | Employee-ownership advocates; Freeman and others | 6,609 ESOPs, 15.1 million participants, over $2 trillion in assets (2023 data: https://www.nceo.org/research/employee-ownership-by-the-numbers [opened]) | Only reaches the employed; concentrates a worker's risk in one firm |
| **Public compute and broad access** | AI Action Plan (NAIRR); OpenAI (access); "Intelligence Curse" authors Drago and Laine (diffuse and democratise AI so people keep leverage: https://intelligence-curse.ai/ [opened]) | Keeps the means of production within reach of individuals | Access is not income if the output of one person's AI is worth little |
| **Steer the technology toward complementing people** | Acemoglu and Johnson; Brynjolfsson ("Turing Trap") [secondary] (memory); Korinek and Stiglitz | Avoids the problem instead of compensating for it | Requires governments to pick research directions; competitors may not follow |
| **Conditional basic income, larger state** | Susskind, *A World Without Work* [secondary] (memory) | Ties support to contribution to protect social solidarity | Who defines contribution |

Scenario frame from the literature: wages can keep rising if automation of harder tasks is slow, and collapse if the set of tasks only humans can do is bounded and full automation is reached; declines can start earlier if automation outruns capital accumulation (Korinek and Suh 2024: https://www.nber.org/papers/w32255 [opened]).

| Study | Design | Work | Health, wellbeing, other | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **OpenResearch Unconditional Cash Study** (Vivalt, Rhodes, Bartik, Broockman, Krause, Miller) | 1,000 low-income adults aged 21-40 in two states got $1,000 a month for three years (Nov 2020-Oct 2023); 2,000 controls got $50 | NBER abstract as revised August 2026 (published in QJE 2026): other income fell about $1,900 a year; labour-market participation down 4.2 points; 1-2 fewer hours a week for recipients and similar for partners; biggest time increase was leisure; no gain in job quality. | **Kenya, general-equilibrium study** (Egger, Haushofer, Miguel, Niehaus, Walker) | One-off transfers of about $1,000 to over 10,500 households in 653 villages; a shock above 15% of local GDP | | Local multiplier 2.4; minimal price inflation; large gains for non-recipients | https://www.nber.org/papers/w26600 [opened] |
| **Alaska dividend** (Jones and Marinescu) | Synthetic control, dividend since 1982 | No effect on employment; part-time work up 1.8 points (17%) | Consistent with local spending offsetting any income effect | https://www.nber.org/papers/w24312 [opened] |
| **Iran 2011 cash transfers** (Salehi-Isfahani and Mostafavi-Dehzooei) | Nationwide payments worth 29% of median household income, replacing energy subsidies | No evidence of reduced labour supply overall; service workers worked more; youth worked less | | https://erf.org.eg/publications/cash-transfers-and-labor-supply-evidence-from-a-large-scale-program-in-iran/ [opened] |
| **Eastern Cherokee casino dividend** (Akee, Copeland, Keeler, Angold, Costello 2010) | About $4,000 a year per adult tribal member | | Children in the poorest households gained a year of schooling by 21; minor crime at 16-17 down 22% | https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/app.2.1.86 [opened] |
| **2021 expanded Child Tax Credit** | Up to $3,600 per child, fully refundable, monthly for six months | Real-world data: small, inconsistent, insignificant employment effects (Ananat et al.: https://www.nber.org/papers/w29823 [opened]). | **Review** (Marinescu 2018) | US and Canadian programmes | A 10% rise in income from unconditional cash lowers labour supply by about 1% | Some health, schooling and crime benefits | https://www.nber.org/papers/w24337 [opened] |

Reading across them:
- **Work:** effects are negative but small in rich countries (roughly an hour or two a week), near zero for universal dividends, and often positive in poor countries. The published OpenResearch estimate is larger than its first draft.
- **Health:** the best US trial found no durable health gain. This cuts against the strongest claims made for cash.
- **Prices:** the only general-equilibrium evidence is from rural Kenya (minimal inflation) and Alaska (no employment loss). Neither tests a permanent, national, tax-financed UBI in a rich country.
- **The gap that matters for this book:** every study measures cash given to people who can still find work. None tests cash in an economy where labour demand has collapsed. Hoynes and Rothstein said in 2019 that pilots would do little to settle the main questions (w25538 [opened]).

### 8.2 Gross cost of a UBI (my arithmetic on the baselines above)

| Design | Gross cost per year | Share of GDP | Share of FY2026 receipts |
|---|---|---|---|
| $1,000 a year to every resident (Sanders-size dividend) | $0.34 trillion | 1.0% | 6% |
| $5,000 once to every adult (Trump promise) | $1.35 trillion one-off (press estimate about $1.25 trillion after exclusions) | 4.1% | 25% |
| $500 a month to every adult | $1.62 trillion | 4.9% | 30% |
| $1,000 a month to every adult | $3.24 trillion | 9.8% | 60% |
| About $16,000 a year (near the one-person poverty line) to every adult | $4.3 trillion | 13% | 80% |
| $2,000 a month to every adult | $6.5 trillion | 20% | 120% |

- Cato's figure for $1,000 a month to every American: $3.7 trillion, about half the federal budget (Semafor URL in 4.3 [opened]).
- **Net cost** depends entirely on the clawback. A UBI paired with an income tax that takes it back from higher earners is arithmetically a negative income tax, and published net-cost estimates for a $12,000 design run to a small fraction of the gross (for example Widerquist's roughly $540 billion a year) [secondary] (memory). In a world where wages have collapsed, though, there is less earned income to claw back from, so net cost converges toward gross cost. The two cases should not be mixed.

### 8.3 What the proposed revenue sources could raise

- **AI-specific taxes are small on today's base.** A 3% tax on AI-service revenue raises $3 billion per $100 billion of revenue (arithmetic). To fund even $500 a month per adult ($1.62 trillion) from a 3% revenue tax would need about $54 trillion of taxable AI revenue, more than today's GDP. Such taxes become material only if AI revenue grows to a large share of the economy, or rates are far higher (Kelly's bill has a 50% excess-profits tax; New Jersey's a 10% token tax).
- **Public equity.** Sanders: 5% of a $7 trillion fund is $350 billion, about $1,000 per resident, but the companies do not currently earn that, and the fund may not sell shares. OpenAI's reported offer: $42.6 billion, about $125 per resident once (arithmetic from 1.4).
- **Corporate profits.** Each additional 10 points of effective federal tax on $4.7 trillion of profits is about $470 billion before behavioural response (arithmetic).
- **Wealth.** Warren-Jayapal 2021 design (2% above $50 million, 3% above $1 billion): at least $3 trillion over ten years per Saez and Zucman. https://www.warren.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/warren-jayapal-boyle-introduce-ultra-millionaire-tax-on-fortunes-over-50-million [opened] Other economists have published much lower estimates on avoidance grounds. [secondary] (memory) Zucman's G20 proposal, a coordinated 2% minimum on about 3,000 billionaires: $200-250 billion a year worldwide; billionaires now pay about 0.3% of wealth in tax. https://taxobservatory.world/publication/a-blueprint-for-a-coordinated-minimum-effective-taxation-standard-for-ultra-high-net-worth-individuals/ [opened]
- **Land.** On Altman's 2021 assumption of $30 trillion of private land, a 1% tax is $300 billion a year before the fall in land prices that the tax itself causes (arithmetic from 5).
- **Altman's combined design** (2.5% of equity value plus 2.5% of land value) was projected by him at $13,500 per adult after a decade, which is roughly the $1,000-a-month row above. It is the only proposal from an AI leader whose stated numbers reach UBI scale, and it does so by taxing all large companies and all land, not AI alone.
- **Consumption.** A broad VAT is the standard large revenue source the US lacks; CBO has scored a 5% VAT in the low trillions over ten years. [secondary] (memory)

### 8.4 Mobility and tax competition

- A wealth or exit tax must deal with emigration (the Warren bill includes a 40% exit tax; URL above [opened]). A token tax must deal with inference served from abroad; New Jersey's bill tries to tax out-of-state processors on in-state use (2.2 [opened]).
- A 15% global minimum corporate tax exists on paper, with a carve-out arrangement for US-parented groups agreed among G7 countries in 2025. [secondary] (memory; OECD page blocked my fetch)
- California's billionaire-tax measure is the live US test of whether mobile wealth can be taxed by one jurisdiction (2.6).

### 8.5 Official modelling of AI and the budget

- **Penn Wharton Budget Model, 8 September 2025:** AI raises the level of GDP by 1.5% by 2035, nearly 3% by 2055 and 3.7% by 2075; the boost to annual productivity growth peaks near 0.2 points in the early 2030s; about 40% of labour income is exposed; a very preliminary estimate is $400 billion lower deficits over 2026-35. The model excludes new products and effects on innovation. https://budgetmodel.wharton.upenn.edu/issues/2025/9/8/projected-impact-of-generative-ai-on-future-productivity-growth [opened]
- **CBO, December 2024,** "Artificial Intelligence and Its Potential Effects on the Economy and the Federal Budget": qualitative; notes that effects on revenue depend on whether income shifts from labour to capital. [secondary] (memory; cbo.gov blocked my fetch) I found no newer CBO, PWBM or Tax Policy Center score of an AI displacement scenario or of any bill in 1.2.
- These official models assume AI is an ordinary productivity gain. None models the case this book is about.

- **European Union.** The AI Act treats recruitment and worker-management systems as high-risk. Commission and Parliament work on algorithmic management and a "quality jobs" agenda exists [secondary] (memory).
- **United Kingdom.** AI Opportunities Action Plan (13 January 2025): 50 recommendations, focused on compute (20-fold expansion of public research compute by 2030), "AI Growth Zones" and skills; it says some jobs will be replaced and gives no estimate. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-opportunities-action-plan/ai-opportunities-action-plan [opened] In January 2026 investment minister Lord Stockwood said the government was weighing a UBI and lifelong learning to "soft-land" declining industries; no formal policy followed that I found. A Morgan Stanley study reported Britain with the largest AI-linked net job losses among countries it examined (8% over 12 months). Fortune 1 Feb 2026 URL in 2.5 [opened]
- **China.** Policy is promotion-first: the "AI+" initiative (launched March 2024) to embed AI across the economy. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence_industry_in_China [opened] A 2026 University College London survey found fewer than 10% of Chinese respondents worried AI would make jobs harder to find, and 96% using AI at work. Same URL [opened] An August 2025 State Council plan set adoption targets for 2027 and 2030, and officials have paired AI promotion with "employment-first" language [secondary] (memory). I found no Chinese redistribution proposal tied to AI.
- **South Korea.** AI Basic Act passed December 2024. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulation_of_artificial_intelligence [opened] President Lee Jae-myung, who built his career on a youth dividend in Seongnam, nearly tripled AI spending to 10.1 trillion won in the 2026 budget, created a 150 trillion won growth fund, raised the corporate tax rate from 24% to 25%, and in July 2025 funded universal consumption vouchers of at least 150,000 won. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Jae-myung [opened] Korea's 2017 cut to automation tax credits is the nearest thing to an enacted robot tax anywhere (Robot tax URL in 6 [opened]).
- **Japan.** An AI promotion law passed in 2025 with a light-touch approach; with a shrinking workforce the official framing is labour shortage, not displacement [secondary] (memory).
- **India.** No standalone AI law; hosted the AI Impact Summit in February 2026 (Regulation of AI URL [opened]). Recent Economic Surveys have warned about AI and labour displacement in a labour-surplus economy [secondary] (memory).
- **Gulf states.** Saudi Arabia created Humain (May 2025; Public Investment Fund 51%, Aramco 49%) to build several gigawatts of AI capacity with Nvidia, AMD and Qualcomm. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humain [opened] The UAE centralised AI governance in a new authority in 2026 (Regulation of AI URL [opened]). These are states with existing sovereign funds and citizen-dividend traditions investing oil wealth into AI capital; relevant as the model the "Intelligence Curse" authors warn about.
- **Cross-country exposure.** IMF (January 2024): about 40% of jobs worldwide and 60% in advanced economies are exposed to AI, roughly half of the latter negatively. https://www.imf.org/en/Blogs/Articles/2024/01/14/ai-will-transform-the-global-economy-lets-make-sure-it-benefits-humanity [opened] ILO (May 2025): one in four jobs has some generative-AI exposure; 34% in high-income countries; clerical work most exposed; women more than men. https://www.ilo.org/publications/generative-ai-and-jobs-refined-global-index-occupational-exposure [opened]
- **Trust.** Stanford's 2026 AI Index reports that in 2025 the US had the lowest trust in its government to regulate AI of the countries surveyed (31%), Singapore the highest (91%). https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report [opened]

## 10. Political economy research

### 10.1 When do elites concede redistribution?

- **Threat of unrest.** Acemoglu and Robinson (2000) argue that nineteenth-century European elites extended the vote, and with it redistribution, to head off revolution, because a promise of future transfers was not credible without a shift in political power. https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/115/4/1167/1820381 [secondary] (memory; page did not render the abstract)
- **Mass war and "compensatory" arguments.** Scheve and Stasavage (2016), using 20 countries over two centuries: inequality alone does not produce high taxes on the rich. They come when the public can argue the state has unfairly favoured the wealthy, above all when ordinary people were conscripted and capital was not. https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691165455/taxing-the-rich [opened]
  - Relevance: the 2026 argument that AI was trained on everyone's work and built with public subsidy is a compensatory argument of this type. Whether it has comparable force is open.
- **The pessimistic case.** If labour is no longer needed, the population loses the leverage (strikes, taxes, conscription) that made those concessions rational. This is the core of the "Intelligence Curse" essays (URL in 6 [opened]) and parallels resource-curse states.

### 10.2 Do automation and trade shocks push voters toward populism?

- **Trade.** Autor, Dorn, Hanson and Majlesi (AER 2020): US districts more exposed to Chinese import competition removed moderates in the 2000s; majority-white or Republican-held areas moved to conservative Republicans, majority-minority or Democratic-held areas to liberal Democrats; exposed counties shifted Republican in presidential votes. https://www.nber.org/papers/w22637 [opened]
- **Robots, Europe.** Anelli, Colantone and Stanig (PNAS 2021): individuals more exposed to industrial robots are more likely to support the radical right in western Europe, and the radical left to a much smaller degree. https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1073/pnas.2111611118 [opened] (abstract via Crossref)
- **Robots, US.** Frey, Berger and Chen (Oxford Review of Economic Policy, 2018) find more robot-exposed areas swung to Trump in 2016, and estimate that Michigan, Pennsylvania and Wisconsin would have gone the other way with slower robot adoption. https://doi.org/10.1093/oxrep/gry007 [secondary] (memory; bibliographic details confirmed, abstract not rendered)
- **Reading for this book.** The historical response of displaced voters has been nativism and protection more than a demand for transfers. 2026 fits partly: the backlash expressed itself as opposition to data centers and to the industry's money, and the winners were Democrats, not the radical right. That is one election in which the incumbent party owned the AI build-out.

### 10.3 How long did major US programmes take?

- **Social Security.** Depression from 1929; by 1931 only 12 states had old-age pension laws; Congress considered many bills without passing any; the Townsend movement ($200 a month for the old) pressed from outside; the Act was signed 14 August 1935 after votes of 372-33 and 77-6. It initially excluded farm and domestic workers, about 65% of Black workers. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Security_Act [opened] About six years from crash to law; first monthly benefits 1940 [secondary] (memory).
- **Medicare.** About 40% of people over 65 lacked health insurance; attempts in 1961-64 failed; it passed in July 1965 after the 1964 landslide changed the vote count. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicare_(United_States) [opened] National health insurance had been proposed by Truman in 1945 [secondary] (memory): twenty years.
- **Guaranteed income.** Nixon's Family Assistance Plan (1969) passed the House and died in the Senate. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_unemployment [opened]
- **Durability.** The 2021 child credit cut child poverty by nearly half and lapsed after one year (section 7). Passing a transfer in an emergency is easier than keeping it.
- **Pattern.** Large programmes passed (a) years after the need was visible, (b) after an election gave one side unusual majorities, and (c) with exclusions that took decades to remove. The 2026 midterm supplies a version of (b) in Congress but not the presidency.

## What cuts against the obvious story

1. **The displacement is not in the aggregate data.** Unemployment was 4.1% and real growth 3.4% in autumn 2026 (1.8). Senate witnesses in July 2026 mostly said tasks are changing, not jobs vanishing (1.3). Policy is being argued on forecasts by AI executives and on public fear. A reader should not assume the crisis the bills describe has arrived.
2. **AI did not decide the midterms.** It ranked last of five tested issues; the economy and corruption dominated (4.1, 4.2). Voters split almost evenly on which party handles AI better, and 43% said neither.
3. **The public is anti-AI but not pro-UBI.** Seven in ten or more expect job losses, yet the last clean UBI poll had 54% opposed, a 2026 survey found a strong preference for earned support over unconditional cash, and a populist Republican (Hawley), a leading AI scientist (Hinton) and reportedly Altman himself have moved away from UBI (4.1, 4.3, 5). "Dividend" and "ownership" framings poll and travel better than "basic income".
4. **The backlash targets the technology's footprint, not its distribution.** What won at the ballot box was limits on data centers (40 of 41), not taxes or dividends. California's billionaire tax was trailing in a Democratic wave (2.4, 2.6).
5. **Redistributive moves came from the right as well as the left.** The administration that pushed deregulation and pre-emption also took equity in Intel and others, floated stakes in AI labs, promised $5,000 cheques and created child investment accounts. Sanders and Trump each floated a public ownership stake in AI firms within days of each other in early June 2026 (1.4-1.6). The partisan map of post-labour policy is not the usual one.
6. **But enacted federal policy moved resources away from the bottom of the income scale.** The one large law of the period cut Medicaid and SNAP and, by CBO's estimate, lowered incomes in the bottom tenth by 3.1% (1.7). The safety net that would catch displaced workers is thinner than in 2024.
7. **Industry money bought less than expected, and industry is split.** The largest pro-industry super PAC lost its biggest general-election bet; one AI lab funded the pro-regulation side; all three leading labs now endorse some public share of returns (4.2, 5). "Big Tech versus the public" is too simple.
8. **Disclosure laws may disclose little.** New York's automation checkbox reportedly produced no AI attributions in a year (2.1). Employers rarely have a reason to label a layoff as AI-caused, and "substantial part" is undefined in California's law.
9. **Cash experiments undercut both camps.** Recipients do not stop working, but the best US trial found a larger work reduction in its published version than first reported and no lasting health benefit (7). And no experiment tests the scenario the book is about.
10. **AI-specific taxes cannot fund the programmes attached to them, yet.** A 3% token tax is rounding error against a UBI until AI revenue is a large share of GDP (8.3). The proposals that reach scale tax all capital and land, which is a much bigger political fight than taxing AI labs.
11. **The federal tax base is the thing at risk.** 86% of receipts come from individual income and payroll taxes (8.1). A fall in wage income shrinks revenue at the moment transfer needs rise, before any new tax is in place.
12. **History says concessions follow leverage, and leverage is what automation removes.** Past redistribution followed credible threats of unrest or mass mobilisation for war (10.1). Displaced voters have more often turned to nativism than to demands for transfers (10.2). The 2026 result is consistent with a peaceful electoral route, but it is one data point, with a veto still in place.
13. **Speed mismatch.** Social Security took about six years from the crash and Medicare twenty from first proposal (10.3). SAG-AFTRA signed away its right to strike over synthetic performers until 2030 (3). The EU delayed its worker-relevant AI rules by sixteen months (9). Institutions are moving on multi-year clocks.
