# 00. Key facts used in the guide

A working digest of the numbers the guide relies on, drawn from the nine research notes in this directory (01 to 09). Each line names the note that carries the source link. All figures are as of 12 November 2026 unless dated. Where the notes mark a figure as unverified, the guide does not lean on it.

## The US economy and labor market (notes 03, 05, 07, 08)

- Nominal GDP $33.0 trillion (Q3 2026, annual rate). Real GDP +3.4% annualized in Q3 2026; +2.3% in 2025.
- Unemployment 4.1% (Oct 2026); peaked at 4.5% in Nov 2025; 4.1–4.3% since March 2026.
- Payrolls 159.3 million; +116,000 in all of 2025 after revisions; about +86,000 a month in 2026.
- Prime-age (25–54) employment rate 80.7%; labor force participation 61.9%.
- Hires rate 3.4% (3.87% average in 2019). Layoffs about 1.6 million a month (1.0%).
- Long-term unemployed 27.6% of the unemployed (about 21% in early 2025).
- Real average hourly earnings −0.7% over the year to Sep 2026 (an energy-led inflation spike; PCE inflation 3.7%, oil up after disruption in the Strait of Hormuz). The Fed raised rates in Sep 2026; 10-year Treasury above 5%; 10-year TIPS 2.66%.
- Productivity +2.3% in 2025, +2.1% over the year to Q3 2026: the long-run average.
- Labor share, nonfarm business (BLS): 53.0% in Q3 2026, the lowest since the series began in 1947. Compensation of employees was 50.9% of gross domestic income in 2025 (53.0% in 2021); wages and salaries 42.0%.
- Corporate profits from current production $4.7 trillion annual rate (Q2 2026), about 14.5% of GDP.
- Federal budget, fiscal 2026: receipts $5.40 trillion (individual income tax $2.85T, payroll taxes $1.81T, corporate $381B, customs $173B); outlays $7.40T; deficit $1.99T. Individual income plus payroll taxes are 86% of receipts.
- About 270 million adults. $1,000 a month each costs $3.24 trillion a year: 9.8% of GDP, 60% of federal receipts.

## Evidence of AI in the labor market (note 03)

- No aggregate displacement visible. Best estimates of the total effect: about 0.1 point of unemployment (Dallas Fed, Goldman Sachs); net drag of 11,000–16,000 jobs a month (Goldman, 2026).
- "Canaries" (Brynjolfsson, Chandar, Chen; ADP payroll data, Aug 2026 revision): employment of 22–25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed occupations is 19% below the path of less-exposed peers (15% a year earlier), through less hiring, not more firing. The gap is 13 points in ADP data and 2 points (not significant) in Census data, and shrinks with education controls. A Danish study (records to December 2024) finds no difference between workplaces that encouraged chatbot use and those that did not.
- Occupations, May 2022 to May 2025 (BLS): computer programmers −31%, data entry keyers −19%, customer service representatives −10% (−284,000), writers −11%, editors −10%, graphic designers −7%; software developers +10%, paralegals +14%, accountants +3%. Call-center employment −27% since Nov 2022 (−116,000).
- Software job postings fell 39% from their peak before ChatGPT launched and are up 33% from the May 2025 low.
- Recent-graduate unemployment 5.2% (Q3 2026), down from 5.6%; ages 20–24 at 7.5%, down from a 9.2% peak in 2025.
- Announced layoffs attributed to AI (Challenger): 54,836 in 2025; 121,401 in Jan–Oct 2026 (20% of the total), but 1,265 in October. Under 1% of the monthly flow of layoffs.
- NY Fed survey (Aug 2026): of AI-using service firms, 4% laid workers off because of AI in six months, 15% hired fewer, 13% hired more.
- At Anthropic the junior share of technical hires fell from 27% to 9% since early 2025.

## AI capability and cost (notes 02, 09)

- METR time horizon (length of task, in human-expert time, done with 50% success): 1 hour (Feb 2025), 4.9 hours (Nov 2025), 12 hours (Feb 2026), 17.4 hours (Apr 2026; 80% horizon 3.1 hours). Doubling about every 4 months since 2023. METR's suite saturates above 16 hours and the chart stopped updating in Sep 2026.
- Remote Labor Index (real freelance projects, human-graded): best agent 2.5% (Oct 2025), 15.8% (Jul 2026), 41.3% (Nov 2026).
- Limits: on 8-hour professional tasks (GDPx, Nov 2026) the best model passes 12%, and near 0% in finance and law; about half of test-passing code changes would not be merged by maintainers; half of business-workflow automations fail; open-ended research attempts were rejected by the original authors; no continual learning.
- Prices per million tokens (input/output): top tier $8–10 / $40–50; flagship $4 / $20; small $0.10 / $0.50. The price of a fixed capability falls about 10–13x a year. The frontier tier got dearer and agents use far more tokens: a frontier agent-hour sells for about $30 and costs $3–6 to serve.
- On machine-checkable tasks the token cost is a few percent of human cost or less (a 16,900-line program estimated at 2–17 engineer-weeks rebuilt for about $150 of tokens).
- AI building AI: over 90% of code at Anthropic is written by its models; METR estimates Anthropic's capability progress at about 2x its earlier rate (range 1.3–3x), about 1.4x across the industry.
- Adoption: 24% of US firms use AI (Census, Oct 2026; 17% a year earlier); about 40% of workers use generative AI at work; 5.3% of work hours involve it. Executives report +0.2% a year of productivity so far.
- Revenue: Anthropic run-rate about $81 billion (31 Oct 2026; $9B at end-2025). OpenAI above $40B (Aug 2026). Combined OpenAI plus Anthropic about $105B in late summer 2026, from $30B in January.
- Capital spending: Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft and Meta spent $376B in 2025 and guide to about $710–730B for 2026. AI investment about 3.6% of US GDP (WSJ estimate, Sep 2026). Free cash flow negative at Amazon and Alphabet; about $250B+ of hyperscaler bonds in 2026.
- Markets: Nasdaq −5.5% on 5 Nov 2026, about 11–15% below its October record; semiconductor index −38% from its June peak; Nvidia's chief executive resigned on 12 Nov 2026.
- Timelines: AI 2027 authors' medians for AI that beats top experts at everything moved from 2030–2035 (Jan 2026) to late 2027–2028 (10 Nov 2026). A 2023 survey of 2,778 AI researchers put a 50% chance on full automation of all occupations by 2116.

## Compute, energy and scaling (note 09)

- AI compute sold, cumulative, in H100-equivalents: 0.3M (2022), 1.6M (2023), 6.7M (2024), 20.3M (2025), 31.6M (mid-2026); 45–65M expected at end-2026. Growth of the stock slowing: 5.3x, 4.2x, 3.0x, 2.6x a year. US about 72%, China about 10%.
- Machine labor capacity (Epoch, Oct 2026): hardware shipped through 2026 could run 16–56 million concurrent frontier agents, or 67–240 million full-time equivalents; through 2027, 140–720 million FTE. The US has about 100 million knowledge workers.
- Rental about $2 per H100e-hour; electricity about 4% of that. Electricity is about 7% of a data center's annual cost.
- Energy: US primary energy use is 9.2 kW per person, about 94 kWh behind each hour worked. A frontier agent-hour uses 1.7–3.4 kWh of electricity.
- Time constants: data center 1–3.6 years; gas turbine delivery 5–7 years; grid connection about 5 years; leading-edge fab 3–5 years; mine 16+ years.
- Binding constraint by year (the note's judgment): 2026 memory chips; 2027 US power and permits; 2028–29 capital and demand; 2030–32 generation, grid and fabs; 2033–36 general industrial capital and materials.
- No large economy has sustained growth above about 15–19% a year (US 1941–43: 17–19%; China's peak 14%).
- Voters sided against data centers in 40 of 41 local measures on 3 Nov 2026.

## Robots and physical work (note 04)

- Humanoid robots sold: 7,000–16,000 in 2025; 19,000–25,000 in the first half of 2026; 85–90% Chinese; most go to research, education and display. Unitree: 73.6% of humanoid revenue from research and education, 9% industrial.
- Paid work is tiny: Agility had $1.8M revenue in 2025 (65,000 fleet hours at nine sites). In January 2026 Tesla said no Optimus robot was doing useful work in its factories.
- Cost: a small Chinese humanoid costs about $10,000 to build; a full-size industrial one sells for about $200,000. Cost per robot-hour today $13–55 (Agility's filed prices), more per unit of work than the person. At plausible 2030 prices ($30,000–80,000): about $5–19 per robot-hour, against US employer cost of $24–48 an hour.
- Speed and reliability: robots are 2–10x slower than people at most manipulation. Best reported generalization: 56% success on long chores in 30 unseen homes (Figure, Sep 2026; 9% without pretraining on human video).
- Driving: Waymo 500,000+ paid driverless rides a week, about 4,000 cars, 82% fewer injury crashes over 271 million miles. US wage-earning drivers: 4.31 million, $229B in wages.
- Industrial robots: 603,307 installed in 2025 (59% in China); stock 5.08 million. Amazon: over 1 million robots and 1.64 million employees.
- Scaling (Epoch, Apr 2026): 1.5–3 million humanoids a year achievable by end-2030, 5–10 million plausible; precision reducers are the tightest part. World car output 96 million a year.
- The target: nine mainly physical occupation groups are 65.1 million US jobs (41.9%) and $3.19 trillion of wages (29.4%).

## Policy and politics (note 07)

- No enacted federal policy addresses AI job loss. The July 2025 budget law cut Medicaid and SNAP by over $1.2 trillion over ten years.
- 2026 bills, none voted on: Sanders (50% of large AI firms' shares into a public fund paying about $1,000 a person a year); Jacobs–Casar token tax; Kelly excess-profits tax; Wyden unemployment insurance expansion (52 weeks at 8.5% unemployment); Hawley–Warner layoff reporting.
- June 2026: President Trump floated federal equity stakes in AI labs; OpenAI reportedly offered 5%. Sep 2026: he promised $5,000 per adult if Republicans held Congress. The federal government holds about 10% of Intel and stakes in mining and chip firms.
- "Trump accounts": $1,000 for each child born 2025–2028; automatic enrollment of 60 million more children announced Oct 2026.
- Midterms, 3 Nov 2026: Democrats won the House and Senate (preliminary counts); divided government for 2027–28.
- Polls: 71% (Pew), 79% (Gallup), 84% (Reuters/Ipsos) expect AI to cost jobs. UBI was opposed 54–45 when last polled cleanly (Pew 2020); in 2026 respondents preferred earned support to unconditional cash seven to one; 65% support a dividend paid from AI profits.
- Unions: the dockworkers won a ban on full automation; most others won notice and consultation. SAG-AFTRA cannot strike over synthetic performers until 2030.
- States: 115 AI laws in 30 states in 2026, mostly disclosure. No robot or token tax enacted anywhere. Five states ban local guaranteed-income programs.
- Cash experiments: OpenResearch ($1,000 a month for three years) cut labor-force participation by 4.2 points and hours by 1–2 a week, with no lasting health gain. Alaska's dividend shows no effect on total employment. The 2021 child tax credit cut child poverty from 9.7% to 5.2%; it lapsed and the rate was 12.4% in 2022.
- Speed of past responses: Social Security came 5.8 years after the 1929 crash; Medicare 20 years after it was first proposed; the CARES Act ($2.2 trillion) passed in 14 days.

## History (note 06)

- Engels' pause (Allen): 1780–1840 output per worker +46%, real wages +12%; 1840–1900 +90% and +123%.
- Handloom weavers: 240,000 (1820) to 10,000 (1860).
- US farm horses and mules: 26.7 million (1918) to 3.1 million (1960).
- Farm share of the US labor force: 73.7% (1800), 40.2% (1900), 8.1% (1960), 1.9% (2000).
- China shock: 2.0–2.4 million jobs; local effects still visible in 2019; displaced workers lose about 25% of earnings long-term.
- Great Depression: unemployment 24.9% (1933), above 14% until 1940.
- Guaranteed income passed the US House in 1970 and 1971 and died in the Senate in 1972.
